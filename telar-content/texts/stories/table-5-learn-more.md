Known as the Place of Banners or the Place of Sacrifice, Pantitlan is a whirlpool located in the eastern lake of the Basin of Mexico.   Pantitlan, an ultimately sacred geological site for the Nahua, was a site for worship of the tlaloque–the water and mountain dieties.  At the end of the festival for the tlaloque, everything used for the festival was offered into the waters of Pantitlan.  Even more so, payments of debts through child sacrificial offerings, to the rain deity Tlaloc, during the festival of Atlcahualo would occur at Pantitlan to ensure rain would come to end a drought.  Then, there was also the festival of Etzalcualiztli, which took place when water levels were high, and hearts were sacrificed to the water to help ensure a steady water supply.
