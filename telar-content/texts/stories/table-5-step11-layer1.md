Lovely Cotinga feathers show how Aztec ritual practice depended on intimate, hands‑on knowledge of the natural world and environment. The Florentine Codex explains that the cotinga's blue‑green feathers were prized because they matched the sacred color of turquoise, revealing how Aztecs linked birds and stones through surface appearance. In the image of feather preparation, the act of plucking, sorting, and arranging feathers demonstrates the craft knowledge that made these materials ritually potent. Cotinga feathers embody the chapter's point that Aztecs were extremely particular about the colors and textures they used because the feathers were not just decorative but carried biological and spiritual significance that artisans activated through careful handling.
